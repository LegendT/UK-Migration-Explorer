## What the data says

**Two figures, one year, one publisher.** In the year ending June 2026 there were
{{asylum/small-boat-arrivals-latest-year}}
[small boat arrivals](/what-the-words-mean/#small-boat-arrivals) and
{{asylum/asylum-applications}} people
[claimed asylum](/what-the-words-mean/#asylum-application). There were fewer than half as many
boat arrivals as asylum claims. Whatever else is true, the boats are not the system.

**But that comparison is looser than it looks, and the looseness is the point.** Arriving and
claiming are two different events, counted separately: an arrival is recorded on the day
someone is detected and a claim on the day it is made, so one person can sit in one year's
arrivals and another year's claims. Someone who arrived on a valid visa and whose
circumstances changed can also make a claim. So dividing
one of these totals by the other does not give the share of claims made by people who came by
boat, and this site does not publish that share, because these two counts cannot produce it.
The two numbers sit side by side above; what they do not do is answer a question about the
same people.

**Small boats are most of one narrower thing.** Of the
{{asylum/detected-unauthorised-arrivals-latest-year}} people detected arriving
without permission in the same year, by boat, hidden in vehicles, at ports or found inland,
small boats are the large majority. That is the sentence the figure actually supports:
the main [irregular route](/what-the-words-mean/#irregular-arrival), not the main route.

## Why the claim is misleading

**The asylum system is a process, and none of it is measured in boats.** In the same year the
Home Office made {{asylum/asylum-initial-decisions-total}} initial decisions. There were
{{asylum/asylum-appeals-backlog}} appeals outstanding at the end of it, at a different
publisher. {{asylum/people-in-receipt-of-asylum-support}} people were receiving
[asylum support](/what-the-words-mean/#asylum-support) on one date, and
{{asylum/returns-asylum-related}} asylum-related [returns](/returns/) took
place. A number counting arrivals on one route answers none of those questions, and the costs,
the waiting times and the outcomes that most arguments are actually about all live in them.

**Protection does not only arrive as an asylum claim.**
{{asylum/total-people-resettled-latest-year}} people were resettled in the year through
organised schemes, which are visas rather than claims, and the separate routes opened for
Ukraine, Hong Kong and Afghanistan have run alongside the asylum system on counts of their
own. Someone counting the asylum system by
counting boats misses the parts of it that are, on the claim's own terms, the orderly ones.

**And the boat figure alone comes in three versions.** The Home Office publishes small boat
arrivals by calendar year, by year ending, and as a running total for the year so far. Those
are three different numbers for what sounds like one thing, so two people can argue about
whether arrivals rose while quoting the same publisher on different bases.

## What the claim gets right

**The route is real, it is large, and it is the one nobody can be sure about.** It is the
main irregular route by a distance, and it rose to a peak of
{{asylum/small-boat-arrivals-peak}} in calendar year 2022. Detected arrivals are also a
floor rather than a total, because an undetected arrival is not in any of these numbers,
which is a genuine reason to treat the figure as a minimum.

**And it is the part of the system a reader can see.** Boats are photographed, decisions are
not. That is a fair reason for the route to dominate the argument, and no reason for one
route's number to stand in for the whole.

## The mirror claim fails too

"Small boats are a rounding error" makes the opposite mistake with the same move. They are the
large majority of detected unauthorised arrivals, and the route brings tens of thousands of
people a year, which no reasonable reading calls a rounding error. Both versions work by
choosing which denominator goes unmentioned.

## Better questions to ask

- Arrivals, claims, decisions, appeals, people supported or returns? These are six different
  counts and only the first is measured in boats.
- Over what period, and on which basis? Calendar year, year ending and year to date give three
  answers for the same route.
- Detected by whom? These figures count detections, so they are a floor rather than a total.

## Source notes

Small boat arrivals, detected unauthorised arrivals, asylum claims, initial decisions, asylum
support, asylum-related returns and resettlement: Home Office, immigration system statistics,
year ending June 2026, published 27 August 2026. Appeals: HM Courts and Tribunals Service and
the Ministry of Justice, as at 31 March 2026, a different publisher on a different cycle.

**This site sums two of the figures on this page rather than reading a published total**, and
both say so in their own records: the year-ending small boat figure, because the Home Office
prints that route by calendar year and by month but not on a year-ending basis, and the
detected unauthorised arrivals total, which the Home Office publishes by quarter and by
calendar year.

**This page does not divide one of these figures by another.** The share of asylum claims made
by people who arrived by small boat is not published, and it cannot be derived from these
counts, because they count different events over the same period rather than the same people.
